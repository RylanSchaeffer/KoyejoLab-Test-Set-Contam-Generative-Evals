The point: loss on the exact solution text going up doesn't mean Math Verify scores go down. 8RFz also notes the darker corollary — if accuracy persists while loss rises, contamination becomes harder to detect via perplexity while still inflating scores, which would make "dilution" the wrong word entirely. Fig. 11's loss↔score correlation is motivation, not proof.

How to respond: Concede the metric gap immediately and without hedging, then close it with data. This is the make-or-break item.

In the window (very feasible): Run Math Verify on every existing overtraining and SFT checkpoint — this is inference-only over checkpoints you already have, embarrassingly parallel, and by far your highest-value compute. Produce accuracy-space analogues of Figs. 3–4. Both outcomes are fine: if accuracy tracks loss, the findings stand and the objection dies; if accuracy decouples from loss, report it honestly as stealth contamination — that's arguably a more alarming and more publishable finding, and reframing Findings 4–5 around it shows scientific maturity rather than damage control. Longer term: Make Math Verify the headline metric wherever you make a performance claim; keep loss as the mechanistic/detection metric.
